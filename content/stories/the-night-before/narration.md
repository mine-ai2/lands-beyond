# Leap In

Philadelphia. July 1, 1776. Tomorrow, the vote.

# Scene

The summer heat hasn't broken, even with the sun gone down. You find yourself in the back room of the City Tavern, the din of the main hall muffled by a heavy oak door. A single candle flickers on the table between you and the man you came to see.

Thomas McKean told you to find him here. Said he'd be alone.

The man across from you is James Wilson — a delegate from Pennsylvania, a lawyer, a man who speaks carefully and commits to nothing. Tomorrow, the Continental Congress votes on independence. Pennsylvania is split. Wilson holds the deciding vote, and he hasn't said which way he'll go.

He swirls his drink, not meeting your eyes.
